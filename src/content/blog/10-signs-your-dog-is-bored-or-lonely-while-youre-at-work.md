Leaving your pup home while you’re at work is inevitable. You hope they spend the day peacefully napping. But have you ever come home to shredded cushions or shoes, pulled-apart garbage, upset neighbours, or an overstimulated dog who bounces off the walls the second you turn the doorknob? Well, you’re not alone, and this is extremely common, especially in younger dogs.

Dogs are social, active animals that thrive on mental stimulation and companionship. When you leave them alone for hours without an outlet for their natural energy, boredom and loneliness quickly take over. In this blog, we cover early warning signs that your dog is bored, lonely, or struggling at home. Let’s get into it.

![](/img/blog/img-6770.jpg)

## The Warning Signs Your Dog Is Struggling at Home
### 1. Destructive Chewing
Dogs explore their world with their mouths, and chewing is a natural way for them to relieve stress. When left alone without a job to do, your dog turns everyday household items into self-soothing items. They might target leather shoes, door frames, drywall, or furniture legs. This behaviour is rarely done out of spite. Instead, it is an outlet for bottled-up tension and boredom.

### 2. Excessive Barking or Howling
If your neighbours mention that your dog cries or barks continuously while you are away, your pup is expressing distress. Barking at every minor noise outside or howling into the empty living room is a common call for interaction. When a lonely dog feels isolated, they use their voice to process their anxiety and call out for company.

### 3. Pacing and Restlessness
When you look at home security clips or observe your dog as you leave the house, you might see them pacing back and forth or continually moving from room to room. Pacing shows that your dog cannot settle their mind or body. This constant motion physically reflects an under-stimulated brain trying to burn off nervous energy.

### 4. Digging Up the Yard or Furniture
Digging is an instinctive behaviour that intensifies when a dog feels bored or restless. Pups left in a backyard might dig under fence lines or tear up flower beds to pass the time. If left indoors, this urge translates into nesting or frantically scratching at carpet corners, couch cushions, and dog beds until the fabric frays.

### 5. Sleeping All Day (Lethargy)
While dogs naturally sleep a lot, there is a distinct difference between peaceful rest and apathy born from depression. If your dog sleeps constantly simply because there is nothing else to do, they can become withdrawn and lethargic. Over time, this lack of mental engagement dulls their mood and overall vitality.

### 6. Needy and Attention-Seeking Behaviours
When you finally return home, a bored or lonely dog clings to your side like velcro. They might constantly nudge your hand with their nose, paw at your legs, whimper, or follow you into every room. This extreme clinginess shows they spent the entire day starving for social contact and human engagement.

### 7. Overly Excited Greetings
A happy tail wag at the door is normal, but jumping out of control, zooming around the house, knocking over furniture, or accidentally urinating upon your arrival indicates an overstimulated pup. When a dog holds back all their energy during a quiet, lonely day, your return triggers an explosive release of built-up frustration.

### 8. Paws or Body Licking (Over-Grooming)
Repetitive behaviours like excessive paw licking, leg chewing, or skin biting are common signs of psychological distress in pets. Just as humans might bite their fingernails when anxious, a bored or lonely dog uses compulsive licking as a coping mechanism. Left unaddressed, this can lead to painful hot spots and skin infections.

### 9. Frequent House Accidents
A house-trained dog that suddenly starts having accidents indoors while you are at work may be reacting to stress. Separation anxiety and boredom frequently upset a dog’s regular physical routine. When a pup becomes distressed or nervous about being alone, they lose their ability to hold their bladder or bowels properly.

### 10. Escape Attempts
When boredom peaks, a dog’s drive to find adventure outside the house increases dramatically. They might attempt to jump back fences, chew through wooden gates, or push through window screens. These dangerous escape attempts show that your dog is actively searching for stimulation and companionship beyond the walls of your home.
